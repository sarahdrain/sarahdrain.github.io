About me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at West Virginia University, where my research focuses on urban and regional economics, healthcare regulation, spatial econometrics, and economic measurement. My dissertation, Economic Incentives and Real Activity: Essays on Policy Transmission and Measurement, examines how policy decisions shape outcomes and how those outcomes can be measured.

My research brings together applied econometric methods and detailed administrative, provider, and spatial data. Projects include the effects of nurse practitioner autonomy on Medicare services and spending; physical investment following Opportunity Zone designation; and household liquidity responses to monetary policy.

Alongside my doctoral research, I work as an economist in Regional Economic Accounts at the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis. I have also conducted research with the Knee Regulatory Research Center and previously worked as a summer associate at RAND Corporation.

My work frequently draws on reproducible data workflows, spatial analysis, and quasi-experimental designs to connect careful measurement with practical policy questions.
